The job · a founder's first inbox session after connecting

Find the email that needs you. Trust the draft. Send.

Fyxer already works inside Gmail and Outlook: it sorts the 300 most recent emails into To respond, FYI and lower categories, and writes drafts in the user's tone, which the user reviews, edits and sends. This lab asks whether a time-poor user finds and trusts that value in session one, and tests one inbox-native answer.

Product value · documented

Urgent threads labelled 1: to respond, drafts from the thread in the user's tone, human edit and send, calendar-checked times (E14, E15, E10, E19).

Growth hypothesis · to test

Some busy users don't find or trust that first draft in session one (A8, A1, A2). Naming the threads in Fyxer's existing welcome email might help. Nothing has been measured.

Instrumentation

Primary: first Fyxer draft sent within 24h of email connection, among exposed new trials. Trial-to-paid is a downstream read (A4).

Trust guardrails

Heavy-edit share, first-draft discards, email disconnects within 72h, support contacts by day 3, trial cancellations.

Built by Devin (an AI agent) at Ayo's direction, for Ayo's review. Every name, thread and draft is invented.

Where this adds value

A ready-to-run activation bet, and how I'd work in the role

The value isn't a claimed uplift, because there isn't one. It's a way to decide faster, and more cheaply, whether one activation idea is worth building. It comes with the evidence, instrumentation and decision rules already worked out.

1 · Why validate before shipping

A 7-day trial leaves little room to miss day one

A user who connects and doesn't recognise a useful draft has already used part of a short trial (E7). That makes the first 24h the earliest point where a change can be seen and measured. Trial-to-paid comes too late and is too sparse to decide on at a realistic sample (A4). The preview also shows why a draft was written, which can build trust or backfire, so it ships only if the guardrails hold. The 48–72h checks come first, to confirm the problem is real (A1, A3) before any build.

2 · What it hands a growth team

Spec, events, test and kill rules

  • PRD for a one-screen preview. It changes no model or pipeline behaviour.
  • Privacy-safe event taxonomy with a payload validator
  • A/B design: primary metric, guardrails, segments, power and runtime, pre-registered ship/stop rules
  • 48–72h validation to check the problem is real before any build

What changes, and what doesn't

System behaviour

Unchanged in both arms. Same categorisation and the same drafts. The treatment doesn't make drafting faster or better, and it doesn't fix a behaviour the product already has.

What the user sees

Control: Gmail or Outlook as documented, with Fyxer's labels, drafts in the thread, and Fyxer's generic welcome email. Treatment: that same welcome email names the To respond threads that already have drafts, why each needs a reply, calendar and setup readiness, and links into each thread. No new surface.

What we measure

Primary: share of exposed trial users who send a first Fyxer draft within 24h of connecting email. Guardrails: heavy-edit share, first-draft discards, disconnects, support contacts and trial cancellations.

                Experiment design ·

                Transparent first-value preview vs the documented flow

                An A/B test, randomised by user at trial start and analysed from the exposure event. The lab ships no baseline rates or results. The calculators below only use numbers you enter.

                Hypothesis

                If newly connected trial users see what was sorted, their first draft with the context behind it, and what setup is holding drafts back, we expect a higher share of them to send a first draft within 24h, because they can find and judge it in the moment (A1, A2), and we expect the trust guardrails to hold. This is a prediction for the test to confirm or reject, not an observed result.

                Primary metric

                First draft sent within 24h. Numerator: exposed users with draft_sent where is_first=true within 24h of integration_connected(email). Denominator: users with experiment_exposed.

                Secondary

                • Time from email connection to first draft_viewed (median, p75)
                • Share with ≥1 draft_sent on 2 or more of trial days 1–3
                • Trial-to-paid (trial_converted). Long-horizon: read, but not a decision metric at this sample size (A4)

                Guardrails (one-sided, pre-registered limits)

                  Segmentation (pre-registered)

                    Post-hoc segments are exploratory only. A ship call never rests on one.

                    Decision criteria

                    1. Invalid if the sample ratio mismatch check gives p < 0.001. Fix assignment or logging first.
                    2. Keep running until the planned users per arm and at least one full week. Early stop only for a significant guardrail breach.
                    3. Stop if any guardrail is significantly worse and above its limit, if the primary is significantly negative, or if the CI's upper bound rules out the MDE.
                    4. Ship if the primary CI's lower bound is above 0 and no guardrail is breached. Size the claim to the lower bound.
                    5. Iterate otherwise: the CI still spans both 0 and the MDE.

                    Sample size and runtime

                    Enter your own baseline. The lab doesn't know Fyxer's.

                    Decision sandbox

                    Enter observed counts to see which pre-registered rule fires. The rule checks below fill in made-up inputs, one per branch. They are not results.

                    MDE and alpha come from the sample size form (defaults 3pp, 0.05). The heavy-edit guardrail is a share of draft senders, with a +10% relative limit.

                    Before building the A/B

                    48–72 hours to decide whether this is worth an experiment

                    The cheapest checks come first. Each has a pre-set threshold, so the go/no-go call can't drift once the data arrives.
